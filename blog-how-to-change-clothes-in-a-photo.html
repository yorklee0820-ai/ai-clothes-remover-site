How to Change Clothes in a Photo Using AI

Change an outfit, colour or fabric in a photo with AI — prompt templates, examples and the mistakes to avoid.

Changing clothes in a photo with AI is one of the most useful edits in fashion retouching: swap a colourway before it exists, preview a styling idea, or fix a lookbook shot without rebooking the studio. Here is how to do it well.

Decide what is actually changing

There are three different edits and they behave differently. A colour change keeps the garment's cut and only alters hue — the easiest and most reliable. A fabric change alters texture as well as colour, so it needs a stronger prompt. A full outfit swap replaces the garment entirely and puts the most demand on the model.

Write the prompt in three parts

The prompts that work best have three clauses: the garment, its properties, and what must stay. For example: “Replace the top with an oversized linen shirt in sand colour; keep the face, pose, hands and background unchanged.” The third clause is the one people forget, and it is the one that prevents the face from drifting.

Match the lighting

If the source photo is shot in warm side light, ask for the new garment to keep that lighting. Without the instruction the model tends to render the new fabric under neutral studio light, which looks pasted on. Mentioning shadows and highlights explicitly is the single biggest quality lever.

Watch the three usual failure points

Check the neckline, the waistline and the hands. These are where a swapped garment most often breaks: a collar that floats, a hem that cuts the body at the wrong point, fingers that merge into a sleeve. Regenerate with a tighter prompt rather than trying to fix them manually.

Use it for real work

Sellers use outfit swaps to show one product in several colourways from a single shoot. Designers use them to test a silhouette before sampling. Photographers use them to rescue a frame where the styling simply did not work. In every case the edit saves a reshoot, which is the actual point.
